GEO: Discovery Optimisation for E-commerce

As search shifts toward AI answers, here's what e-commerce brands need to rethink about discoverability.

GEO August 2, 2026 · 6 min read

A growing share of product research now starts inside an AI assistant, not a traditional search box. For e-commerce brands, that changes what "being found" actually means, and it's the gap I've been closing for clients like Export Club, Fragrance Nook, and Diva Dolls Boutique.

What GEO actually optimises for

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of structuring your content so AI systems can accurately understand, summarise, and cite it, whether that's a chatbot answering "what's the best fragrance gift under $50" or an AI shopping assistant comparing products.

Unlike traditional SEO, GEO cares less about exact keyword matching and more about clarity, factual accuracy, and how easily your content can be lifted and reused in an AI-generated answer.

Where most product pages fall short

  • Vague descriptions. "Premium quality, great for any occasion" tells an AI system nothing useful to cite.
  • Missing specifications. Size, materials, ingredients, and compatibility details are exactly what AI systems pull into comparison answers.
  • No structured data. Without Product and Review schema, AI crawlers have to guess at price, availability, and rating.
  • Thin FAQ content. Direct question-and-answer formatting is one of the easiest wins for AI visibility.

A practical GEO framework for product pages

1. Answer the question before it's asked

Add a concise, factual summary near the top of each product page: what it is, who it's for, and what makes it different. This is the block most likely to be lifted into an AI answer.

2. Structure specifications clearly

Use labelled lists or tables for size, material, ingredients, or compatibility rather than burying details in paragraph text.

3. Add real, specific FAQs

Replace generic FAQs with the actual questions customers ask in reviews and support tickets. These map closely to what people ask AI assistants too.

4. Implement Product, Review, and FAQ schema

This gives AI systems (and traditional search) a structured, unambiguous source to pull from instead of having to infer details.

If an AI assistant can't confidently summarise your product from your page alone, it will recommend a competitor's instead.

Measuring GEO impact

GEO results don't show up neatly in a single dashboard yet, so I track a combination of signals: branded search growth, referral traffic from AI platforms where visible, and manually testing how products are described when asked directly through tools like Claude and other AI assistants.

The takeaway

GEO isn't a replacement for SEO. It's an extension of the same principle that's always mattered: make it effortless for a system, human or AI, to understand exactly what you're offering.
